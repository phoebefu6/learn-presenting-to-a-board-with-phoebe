The RAG rule bench

Session 3 chose which numbers belong in the pack. Tonight we test the colour that sits next to them. The bench below takes 30 real months of one public number with a published target, and gives every month a red, amber or green status under a rule you choose. Change the target, the tolerance band, the rule or the trend window, and watch the same months change colour. Then find the green months that were getting worse, and see what a board misses when it is shown only the latest month.

Part 1 · covers the data, the target and the three rules

How a rule turns a number into a colour

6 min live

A RAG status is not a measurement. It is a number passed through a rule: compare it with a target, allow some tolerance, perhaps look at its direction. Change any part of the rule and the colour changes while the number stays exactly where it was.

LiveThe data, and why it can be trusted this far2 min▶

The rows come from NHS England's Monthly A&E Time Series, published 8 October 2026: the Performance sheet, column "Percentage in 4 hours or less (all)", for England and all A&E types. NHS England's site terms allow reuse under the Open Government Licence (read at source). The bench ships June 2023 to September 2026 and rates April 2024 onwards, so a 9-month trend always has history.

Two checks before trusting it. The series starts in June 2023 because, from May 2019 to May 2023, fourteen trusts stopped reporting four-hour data during a field test of new standards (NHS England's statistics page, read at source), which makes earlier months hard to compare. And the same file, weighted by attendances, gives 74.9% for 2025-26 when the build script checks it; the National Audit Office reports 74.9% for that year (read at source). The rows agree with the auditor.

LiveWhich target? There are two, and the second one moved2 min▶

The formal standard is 95% of patients within four hours. NHS England's medium term planning framework says it is "over a decade since the service delivered" it, and the NAO says it was last met nationally in 2013-14, at 95.7% (both read at source). Beneath it sit interim milestones from NHS England's planning documents: 76% by March 2024, a minimum of 78% in March 2025, 78% again in March 2026, and every trust at 82% by March 2027 (each read at source).

Each milestone is set for March. Comparing every month of the year with it is a reporting convention, and the bench labels it so. A board paper that says "red against target" has already made two choices: which target, and when it applies.

Self-studyRAG is a convention, not a standard3 min▶

No statute or code tells a board how to colour a KPI. NHS England's own improvement guide, Making data count (2019, read at source), says its first edition "exposed the limitations" of two-point comparisons and RAG reports, and shows an indicator varying randomly around its target that "would flip between red and green". It also grants RAG summaries their appeal: they are concise. The UK's Infrastructure and Projects Authority calls its delivery ratings a "snapshot in time" and moved from a five-point scale to three (Annual Report 2023-24, read at source).

Teach the disagreement honestly. A colour is fast to read and boards ask for it; a colour also throws away the direction, the distance from target and the noise. Nobody has shown that one rule leads to better board decisions than another. The bench cannot settle that. It shows how much the choice moves the picture, so you can choose on purpose and say what you chose.

Build-along · the bench

Thirty real months, six rules, and yours

★ 26 min · the main event

The bench opens on the interim milestone with a 2-point tolerance band and a 6-month trend window. Work down the steps in order and say out loud what changed before you move on. Every number below is one the bench prints; if your screen shows something else, trust the screen and tell me.

Read the bench as loaded. Interim milestone, a 2-point band, a 6-month window. Look at the strip under the chart: one cell per month, mostly amber and red.

★ Step 1 · the default ruleUnder this rule, 30 rated months: 3 green, 13 amber, 14 red. 0 months are green while the 6-month trend is down half a point or more.

Select "Formal target, month by month". 95% every month, no tolerance. Notice the chart: the target sits far above it.

★ Step 2 · the formal target3 green, 13 amber, 14 red becomes 0 green, 0 amber, 30 red. Every setting on the bench with the formal target prints 0 green.

Select "Interim target, month by month", then drag the tolerance band from 0 to 5 points, one step at a time. Write down the green count at each step.

★ Step 3 · the band, step by step0 points: 0 green 1 point: 1 green 2 points: 3 green 3 points: 8 green 4 points: 16 green 5 points: 21 green Green while declining: 0, 0, 0, 0, 3, 7

Select "Trend only, 6 months". The rule now ignores the target and looks only at direction.

★ Step 4 · trend only17 green, 2 amber, 11 red. 0 green while declining, by construction: a trend rule cannot be green while falling. The same months against the interim milestone, no band: 0 green.

Select "Level and trend together". Green only when the level and the trend are both green.

★ Step 5 · combined3 green, 10 amber, 17 red. 0 green while declining.

Select "Green-by-rule". The anti-lever: interim milestone and a 5-point band. Count the ▼ marks under the strip.

★ Step 6 · the anti-lever21 green, 9 amber, 0 red. 7 months are green while the 6-month trend is down half a point or more. 21 is the most greens any setting prints.

Back to "Interim target, 2-point tolerance". Tick "Latest month only". The break button: what a board sees when the paper reports one month.

★ Step 7 · the break buttonLatest month only. A board shown September 2026 alone sees one RED: 74.4% against the 82.0% milestone. The series it does not see: 3 green, 13 amber, 14 red over 30 months, and -2.75 points over the last 6 months.

Keep the break on. Select "Trend only, 6 months", then set the trend window to 9 months, then to 3. Watch the latest month's colour.

★ Step 8 · the window is a choice6 months: September 2026 is RED, -2.75 points. 9 months: September 2026 is GREEN, +0.52 points. 3 months: September 2026 is RED, -0.66 points.

Untick the break. Pick the rule you would defend for one KPI in your own pack, and write the sentence a director could read in the paper.

★ Step 9 · your rule, in one sentenceYour numbers will differ. Write down: target: (formal or interim, and which) tolerance band: (points, and who agreed it) trend window: (months, and why that many) "Green means ... ; amber means ... ; red means ..."

Read it in this order. The verdict line first, then the strip, then the comparison table. The strongest lesson is not any one count: it is that one honest series prints 0 greens or 21 greens depending only on the rule, and that the rule which prints the most greens is also the one that hides months getting worse. The project-status version of this, where amber means what the team agreed it means, is in AI Project Management, The status report ↗; this bench is about a KPI over time.

Part 2 · covers what the rules show

The band and the window are decisions

6 min live

Every number on the bench is real, and every colour is honest under its own rule. That is exactly the problem a director faces: two papers can describe the same performance in green and in red, and neither has lied. The difference sits in the tolerance band and the trend window, which rarely appear in the paper at all.

LiveA wide band does not just add greens3 min▶

From 0 to 3 points, widening the band turns reds into ambers and a few ambers into greens, and none of those greens is a falling month. At 4 points, 3 green months are falling; at 5 points, 7 are. Under the 5-point band the falling greens include January 2025 at 73.0%, down 2.21 points on six months earlier, and February 2025 at 73.5%, down 2.87. Hover any cell on the strip to read its month, share and change. A director reading green in those months would have had no reason to ask.

Practitioners call a status that is green outside and red inside a watermelon. It is slang; no governance body defines it. The bench counts the concrete version: green under the rule while the trailing trend is down half a point or more.

LiveTrend-only answers a different question3 min▶

Trend-only prints 17 greens on months that, against the interim milestone with no band, print 0. It is not lying: it answers "is it getting better?", not "is it good enough?". A board needs both answers, which is why the combined rule exists, and why its 3 greens match the level rule's 3 at the same band. Combining did not add greens; it turned 3 of the level rule's ambers red, because those months were falling.

Real world

The colour flips when the number does not. NHS England's Making data count (2019, read at source) shows an indicator that varies randomly around its target and, in a RAG report, "would flip between red and green" from month to month. Its answer is a statistical process control chart, which separates noise from a real shift. The bench does not teach that method; it is the strongest alternative to RAG named by a public body, and worth a self-study hour.

Part 3 · covers the anti-lever, the break button and the bench's limits

Green-by-rule, one month, and what the bench cannot tell you

6 min live

A bench that can only report good news measures nothing. This one has two ways to make the picture worse: the anti-lever picks the rule that prints the most greens, and the break button shows the board one month instead of the series.

LiveThe anti-lever, honestly sized2 min▶

Green-by-rule is not an invented trick. A band of several points below an interim milestone is the kind of tolerance a reporting team can propose with a straight face, and each step to it was reasonable on its own. Of the 108 settings the bench can take, it prints the most greens, 21, and it is the only preset with green months that were falling, 7 of them. The defensible claim is that one: the rule that maximises green is the rule that hides deterioration. It is not that a wide band is always wrong; a band agreed in advance, written in the paper and applied the same way every month is a legitimate choice the board can test.

LiveThe break button: right colour, missing story2 min▶

Under the default rule September 2026 is red, and a one-line status that says "red" is accurate. What it leaves out is that the number fell 2.75 points in six months, from 77.1% in March 2026. Under green-by-rule the same one line says amber. With a 9-month trend window, trend-only calls it green, because December 2025 was lower still. One month plus one rule hands the board a colour and keeps the direction for the paper's author. Show the series, or at least the trend beside the colour, and say which window you used.

Self-studyWhat the bench leaves out4 min▶

The half-point trend threshold, the window lengths and the band steps are the bench's choices, labelled on the widget, not anyone's standard. A two-point difference between one month and another is a crude trend; it can flip on a single unusual month, which is the noise problem Making data count describes. The series is national; a trust or a ward could look very different. And no rule on the bench is the right one: the evidence that any RAG rule improves board decisions was not found for this build.

What survives these limits is the reporting lesson, and it fits the governance texts from session 1: directors must act "on a fully informed basis" (OECD 2023, V.A, read at source), and the board presents a "fair, balanced and understandable" assessment (FRC Code 2024, Principle N, read at source). A rule nobody wrote down cannot be tested by anyone.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The same 30 real months print 0 greens under one rule and 21 under another. What changed?

Every run reads the same rows from one published file, and nothing on the bench is random. The colour is the number passed through a rule; change the rule and the colour moves while the number stays put.

2 · Under green-by-rule, 7 months are "green while declining". What does that count mean?

No rated month comes near 95%, and none is missing. The count is green status AND a trailing 6-month fall of at least 0.5 points: a falling number painted green. The default rule has none.

3 · With the break on, September 2026 is red with a 6-month window and green with a 9-month window under trend-only. What is the right reading?

Over six months the share fell 2.75 points; over nine it rose 0.52, because December 2025 was lower still. Neither window is the true one. The professional move is to say which you used and let the board see the series.
