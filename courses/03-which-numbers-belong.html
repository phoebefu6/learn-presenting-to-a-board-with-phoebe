Learn Presenting to a Board with Phoebe · Session 3 of 6

Which numbers belong

A data leader can bring thirty numbers to a board, and every one of them is true. The board needs three, each with the target it is measured against, the direction it is moving and the tolerance agreed before anyone saw the result. Tonight we sort the numbers with three tests, give each survivor its context, and draw the line every board paper has to respect: you may leave out detail that does not help oversight, but never a bad truth the board needs.

Part 1 · covers board KPIs versus operational detail, and the three tests

A board number answers an oversight question

5 min live

Operational detail answers a management question: is the build on schedule this sprint, how many fields are mapped, which depot is behind. A board number answers an oversight question: is the strategy working, is a principal risk inside the appetite the board set, did the thing we approved deliver what it promised. Both kinds are true and both matter. They belong in different documents, read by different people.

LiveThe three tests, in the order a director would apply them3 min▶

1. Does a director need it to decide or to oversee? Not "is it interesting" and not "did my team work hard for it". If the number does not serve the decision on the cover sheet, or one of the board's standing oversight questions, it fails here.

2. Would it change a vote, or a question worth asking? Ask what a director would do differently if the number were twice as good or half as good. If the answer is "nothing", it is context, not a board number.

3. Can they act on it? A board acts by approving, challenging, asking for assurance or escalating. Fields mapped per sprint is management's to act on; the share of accounts whose lists agree tells the board whether the record it paid for is working.

The tests are a teaching device, not a standard. They put into practice what the FRC's 2024 guidance asks of board papers in paragraph 78 (session 2): accurate, clear, comprehensive and up to date, with a summary and a statement of what is expected of the director.

LiveSunniva's three numbers, and why each survived2 min▶

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. Accounts whose three lists agree on name, address and contact: today fewer than half of 2,140. It is the direct measure of what the board is buying, and it will move first. Credit notes for wrong-address deliveries: about 310 last quarter. It is the cost the record is meant to remove, Halvard Lind can check it against the ledger, and Leonie will recognise it from the depot floor. Spend against plan: €1.4 million over two years, of which the first year is €1.08 million (€900,000 platform and build plus €180,000 depot and sales time). It is the number the board is approving.

Fields mapped, matching-rule tests, tickets closed and the sprint plan all failed test 3: they are management's to act on. They go in an appendix, and the paper says in one line that it is there.

Self-studyKPI trees and metric definitions2 min▶

How a KPI decomposes into the drivers below it, and how to write a metric definition someone else can recompute, are taught in Data Literacy, Metrics and KPI trees ↗. The board sits at the top of the tree. The drivers lower down are where management works, and they are usually where the appendix numbers come from.

Real world

Directors say packs are too operational. A 2017 survey by ICSA and Board Intelligence, reported by Hodge (2018) on Mondaq, found 68% of respondents thought board packs too operational, and nearly three-quarters thought them too long. These are reported survey figures, not a measurement of any one board, but the direction is consistent: the commonest fault is too much management detail, not too little.

Part 2 · covers trend, target and tolerance, materiality and risk appetite

A number a director can oversee comes with three companions

5 min live

"On-time delivery 93.8%" cannot be overseen. Is that good? Is it improving? Should anyone have told the board? A director can only judge it beside a target, a trend and a tolerance band, and the tolerance has to be agreed before the result is known. Set afterwards, a tolerance is a story told to fit the number.

LiveTarget and tolerance, set before the result3 min▶

The target says what good looks like. The trend says which way the number is moving, over a window you name. The tolerance band says how far below target the number may fall before management must tell the board, and it is the part most packs leave out. Constructed Fernhollow Freight example: on-time delivery is amber at 93.8%, inside the band, but it has fallen 1.3 points since June. A director who sees only "amber" learns nothing; one who sees the band and the trend can ask whether November is the floor or the next step down.

How much the colour depends on these choices is session 4's bench. On 30 real months of NHS England's four-hour A&E figure, the same rows print 0 greens or 21 greens depending only on the target and the band chosen. The data did not change. The rule did. Write the rule into the paper, under the number, before the board ever sees a colour.

Self-studyRisk appetite in a sentence, and materiality as a judgement3 min▶

Under the UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC, 2024, read at source), Principle O gives the board the job of determining the nature and extent of the principal risks the company is willing to take to reach its long-term objectives, and Provision 28 asks the board to carry out a robust assessment of the company's emerging and principal risks. Risk appetite is that determination written down. A tolerance band is risk appetite applied to one number.

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example, one sentence Sunniva could propose: "The board accepts depot staff time during the cut-over up to the €180,000 budgeted in year one; it does not accept a launch with the CRM account lists missing." The first half is a tolerance; the second is a line the board will not cross.

Materiality is a judgement, not a formula. The board's attention belongs on what could change a decision, and what counts depends on the company, the decision and the moment. Because it is a judgement, the paper should state its threshold ("we report any deviation above 5% of plan", for example, in constructed numbers) so a director can disagree with the threshold rather than discover it later.

Self-studyProvision 29, and why data leaders should care2 min▶

The 2024 Code changed Provision 29. The board monitors the company's risk management and internal control framework and, in the annual report, now includes a declaration on the effectiveness of its material controls. The change applies to accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026, so the first declarations appear in 2027 annual reports (FRC, 2024, read at source).

Many material controls run on data: controls over financial and operational reporting, over customer records, over the figures in the board pack itself. If your board makes that declaration, the numbers you bring are evidence the board leans on, and a director like Tomasz will ask how they are produced and checked. Constructed Fernhollow Freight example: Fernhollow is not listed and follows the Code only as good practice, so Provision 29 binds it only if its board chooses to adopt it. The question about how the numbers are checked comes anyway.

Real world

Same rows, different colours. Session 4 runs NHS England's monthly four-hour A&E series, England, all A&E types, against its published targets. With the formal 95% target, every one of 30 rated months is red; with the interim milestone and a generous band, 21 are green. Every number is real and every colour is honest under its own rule, which is why the rule belongs in the paper.

Part 3 · covers withholding detail versus withholding truth

Cut the detail, never the bad news

5 min live

Parts 1 and 2 cut thirty numbers to three. That is the professional norm, and it has a limit. "Withhold" means leaving out detail that does not help oversight. It never means leaving out a material bad truth, and the reason is the board's own duty: directors act on what the paper shows them, so a paper that hides a problem takes away the thing their judgment depends on.

LiveWhy the norm is "fully informed", in four sources3 min▶

The G20/OECD Principles (2023, read at source) say board members should act "on a fully informed basis" (V.A). The UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC, 2024, read at source) asks the board to present a "fair, balanced and understandable" assessment of the company's position and prospects (Principle N), and makes the chair responsible for directors receiving accurate, timely and clear information (Principle F). The Companies Act 2006, section 174 (read at source), holds each director to reasonable care, skill and diligence, and that care is exercised through the paper in front of them.

Put together: cutting detail helps a director use their time on what matters. Cutting a material bad truth leaves them unable to do the job the law gives them, and when it surfaces later, the question is not only "what went wrong" but "why were we not told".

Live"Never bring a problem without a solution": a saying, and its limit2 min▶

Most managers have heard it. It is opinion, with no traceable origin, and it pulls in two directions. A problem that arrives with options and a recommendation is far more useful to a board than a bare alarm. But a problem held back until a solution exists reaches the board late, and sometimes not at all.

The FRC's 2024 guidance names, among risk factors for poor board decisions, "a tendency to bring matters to the board for sign-off rather than debate" (Figure 3, read at source). A board that only ever sees solved problems is being asked to sign off, not to oversee. The workable line: bring the problem as soon as it is material, with what you are doing about it so far, and say plainly which parts are not yet solved. The evidence does not settle how long to wait; your board's appetite and the size of the problem do.

Self-studyRAG and the limits of a colour2 min▶

NHS England's improvement guide, Making data count (2019, read at source), sets out the limits of red, amber and green reports: a measure varying randomly around its target can swap between red and green from month to month with nothing real changing, and a two-point comparison cannot tell noise from a shift. Its alternative is the statistical process control chart, named here and not taught. Practitioners call a status that is green outside and red inside "watermelon" reporting; it is slang, and no governance body defines it. Session 4 counts the concrete version on real rows.

Talking about data risk upstairs, in the governance frame rather than the KPI frame, is taught in Data Governance, Governance as advantage, talking to the board ↗.

Real world

Directors notice when bad news is softened. In a 2024 Board Intelligence survey, reported by Comsure (2025), 42% of respondents thought management were not upfront enough about bad news. It is a vendor's survey reported second-hand, not a measured rate for any one board, but a director who has been surprised once reads every later paper looking for the next surprise.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Which of these belongs in Sunniva's board pack, rather than an appendix or the management report?

Credit notes pass all three tests: a director needs it to oversee the outcome, a change in it could change a vote, and the board can act on it. Fields mapped and test counts are management's to act on. Bringing every number is not transparency; it buries the three that matter, and the appendix line keeps the detail available.

2 · What may "withhold" mean in a board paper, and why?

OECD V.A asks directors to act on a fully informed basis, FRC Principle N asks for a fair, balanced and understandable assessment, and the s174 care duty relies on the paper. Detail can move to an appendix; a material problem cannot leave the paper. Holding a problem back until it is solved invites the sign-off rather than debate the FRC warns against.

3 · What does the 2024 change to Provision 29 of the UK Corporate Governance Code add, and from when?

Provision 29 now asks the board to declare on the effectiveness of its material controls, applying to accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026. The Code says nothing about RAG statuses or band widths, and it places duties on the board, not on a head of data.
