Learn Presenting to a Board with Phoebe · Session 5 of 6

The questions you will be asked

The ten minutes on the agenda are mostly not yours. A board item for decision is short on presenting and long on questions, and the questions are where directors do their job: testing that what the paper says is true, challenging the choice, and asking what it means for where the company is going. Tonight you learn to hear which kind of question is coming, read the likely ones straight off your own paper, answer "I don't know" without damage, and talk to the chair before the pack goes out. By the end you have five questions with written answers for your own item.

Part 1 · covers assurance, challenge and strategic questions, and where they come from

Three kinds of question

5 min live

A director's question is easier to answer once you hear what kind it is. Some ask how anyone knows the paper is true. Some ask why this option and not another. Some ask what the decision means for the company in three years. Each kind wants a different answer, and each traces back to the same line of the governance code.

LiveThe three kinds, and why they are only a teaching aid3 min▶

Assurance asks "how do we know this is true, and under control?" The answer is evidence: the source of a number, who checked it, what control would catch a failure. Challenge asks "why this and not that, and what if you are wrong?" The answer is the alternatives you weighed and why they lost, and what you would do if the main risk landed. Strategic asks "what does this mean for where we are going?" The answer is the long-term effect, in the board's language rather than the project's.

Be clear about the status of this split. It is a teaching typology: a way to sort questions so you can prepare for them. No governance code or statute lists these three kinds, and a real question often mixes two. Its anchor is the UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC, 2024, read at source), Principle H, which asks non-executive directors to provide "constructive challenge", strategic guidance, specialist advice, and to hold management to account. The three kinds are those jobs, pointed at your paper. The duties behind them are in session 1.

Why non-executives ask so much: the OECD Principles' commentary on V.F (2023, read at source) notes that non-executive board members do not have the same access to information as key managers. They are not in the corridor, the stand-up or the steering group. The questions are how they close that gap, and the paper is where they start.

LiveWhy challenge is not hostility2 min▶

The FRC's 2024 guidance to the Code (paragraph 75, read at source) says executives should "welcome constructive challenge". That is the professional norm, and it has a reason: a director who votes for €1.4 million without testing it has not exercised the independent judgment or the care the Companies Act asks of them (sections 173 and 174). A sharp question from Leonie is her doing her job on your behalf, because a decision that survived challenge is a stronger decision for you to deliver.

In practice that changes your posture. Answer the question asked, not the one you hoped for. Agree with what is right in it before you add what is missing. If a challenge exposes a weakness the paper did not name, say so plainly; the board must act on a fully informed basis (OECD V.A), and a weakness found in the room after being left out of the paper costs far more trust than one named on the cover sheet.

The FRC guidance also offers boards a list of example questions for significant decisions (paragraph 30, read at source); one asks whether the board has a clear idea of the success criteria for the decision. Read that list once before you write. It is the board's own preparation, published.

Self-studyThe questions after a RAG status: "What rule makes it green?"3 min▶

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. At the last meeting Tomasz Wexley looked at the green on-time-delivery status in the KPI pack and asked one thing: "What rule makes it green?" That is an assurance question in its purest form. He was not doubting the number; he was asking how anyone would know whether green meant good.

Any colour in your paper invites the same family of questions: what is the target, how wide is the tolerance band, over what window is the trend measured, and has the rule changed since last quarter. On the session 4 bench, the most generous rule on the same real months printed 21 green. A director who has seen that will ask about your rule, so write it under the colour before anyone has to.

Real world

The information gap is structural. The OECD Principles (2023, Chapter V, read at source) treat board members' access to "accurate, relevant and timely information" as a condition of doing the job, and the V.F commentary names the reason non-executives need it: they do not see what managers see. Every question you are asked is partly a request for information the paper could have carried.

Part 2 · covers anticipating questions from your own pack

Read your paper as each director

5 min live

Most board questions are written by the paper before anyone opens their mouth. Every number invites "compared with what?". Every risk invites "how would we know early?". Every option you rejected invites "why not?". If you read your own cover sheet slot by slot, the question list nearly writes itself.

LiveThe question list, built from the pack3 min▶

Print your paper. Read it three times, each time as one director, with a pen. On every number write "compared with what?" and check whether the paper answers it with a target, a trend or a baseline. On every risk write "how would we know early?" and check whether there is a named signal and an owner. On every rejected option write "why not?" and check there is one line of reason. On every claimed benefit write "how measured?", and where the honest answer is "not yet", say so in the paper.

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. Sunniva's memo says fewer than half of the 2,140 accounts ordered in the last year agree on name, address and contact across the three lists. Tomasz will ask how that was counted. Her memo also says key-account retention is plausible but not measured. Halvard will ask what the board is buying if retention is not the case. Both questions are already in the paper; neither should land as a surprise.

LiveWrite the answers down the day before2 min▶

A prepared answer is two sentences: the answer, then the number and where it comes from. Write it, do not just think it, because writing shows you which answers you do not actually have. How to rehearse those answers aloud, and how to handle the question you did not see coming, is taught in Public Speaking 06, Rehearsal and the questions ↗. The board-specific part is only this: tag each question with its kind and its likely asker, so you rehearse the evidence for Tomasz and the alternatives for Leonie, not one generic answer for everyone.

Self-studyData-protection questions go to the committee first2 min▶

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. One shared customer record means one place holding names, addresses and contacts for every account. Tomasz's audit and risk committee will want to test the controls around it: who can see the record, how a wrong change is caught, how the record fits the company's data-protection obligations. Those are assurance questions with a natural home in the committee, which reports to the board.

Ask Delphine Corwen whether the item goes to audit and risk before the December board. If it does, the committee's questions become your rehearsal, and the board will often hear a one-line committee report instead of reopening every control. How to talk about data risk upstairs is taught in Data Governance e4, the board talk section ↗.

Real world

Ask the board's questions before the board does. The FRC's 2024 guidance (paragraph 30, read at source) publishes example questions for boards facing significant decisions, among them whether the board knows the success criteria for the decision. Reading your paper against that list is the same exercise as reading it as each director, with the regulator's own prompts.

Part 3 · covers "I don't know", the action log, and pre-wiring the chair

"I don't know", and the call before the pack goes out

5 min live

You will be asked something you cannot answer. That is normal and survivable. What does damage is a guess: a number said in the boardroom enters the minutes, and a wrong one comes back at the next meeting with your name beside it. The other half of tonight is the conversation that makes fewer surprises possible: a short call with the chair before the pack goes out.

Live"I don't know", said well3 min▶

A good "I don't know" has four parts: what you do know, what you will find out, by when, and that you will confirm it in writing. "I don't know the exact figure for the northern depots. I know the company-wide figure is about 310 credit notes last quarter. I will have the depot split to Delphine by Friday, in writing." That answer gives the director something now, a date, and a written record they can check.

The follow-up then goes on the action log. The ICSA/CGI minute-taking guidance (2017, read at source) asks minutes to record actions with an owner and a date, and to keep them in a separate actions schedule reviewed under matters arising at the next meeting. Your promise becomes a line Delphine will read out in January. Keep it, and the board learns that your "I don't know" is reliable, which is worth more than any single answer.

If the answer you do not have is material to tonight's vote, do not paper over it. Offer the chair a choice: take the decision with the gap named, or defer the item. Saying how sure you are, in words a board can act on, is taught in Decision Intelligence 04, Uncertainty you can promise ↗.

LivePre-wiring the chair2 min▶

Before the pack goes out, ask for fifteen minutes with the chair, with the company secretary if they wish. The call has three purposes: confirm what the item needs from the board and that its type is right; hear which questions the chair expects; and check readiness, meaning whether the board has enough in the paper to decide on the night. It is not lobbying for the vote. The chair's job is a well-run decision, not your project, and a call that sounds like canvassing will be treated as one.

Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. Sunniva's call with Ingrid Ashdown and Delphine Corwen confirms "for decision", surfaces one expected question (Leonie on depot staff time at cut-over), and agrees that the retention claim is labelled "not measured" on the cover sheet rather than left implied. How pre-wiring works across a wider set of stakeholders is taught in Stakeholder Management 05 ↗; this is the board-specific version, with one person and one purpose.

Real world

Bad news is where trust is lost. In a Board Intelligence survey (2024, reported by Comsure; the vendor's own survey, not read at source), 42% of respondents said management was not upfront about bad news. Treat that as a reported figure, not a measurement you can quote as fact. The norm behind it is firmer: directors must act on a fully informed basis (OECD V.A), the board's assessment must be fair, balanced and understandable (FRC Principle N), and a director's duty of care (Companies Act 2006, s174) assumes what they are shown can be relied on. Leaving out operational detail is fine; leaving a material problem for a director's question to uncover is not.

Build-along

Five questions, answered

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Take the paper you drafted in session 2 and the numbers you chose in session 3. You will list the questions it invites, sort them by kind and by director, keep the five most likely, write a two-sentence answer for each with its number and source, write the honest answer for the one you cannot yet answer, and plan the call with the chair. Sunniva's version is on the left of every step; yours goes beside it.

List ten questions your paper invites. Go slot by slot: every number, every risk, every rejected option, every claimed benefit. Do not filter yet.

★ Step 1 · ten questions1 Why this, and why now? 6 How were the 2,140 accounts counted? 2 Compared with what (310 notes)? 7 Why not make the CRM the master? 3 How would we know early (CRM)? 8 What does cut-over cost the depots? 4 Why not a one-off clean? 9 Will this keep our key accounts? 5 Who checks the 310 figure? 10 Is the board ready to decide tonight? (Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. Your list will differ.)

Tag each one: assurance, challenge or strategic. If a question is two kinds, tag the one its asker cares about more.

★ Step 2 · tag the kindAssurance: 2, 3, 5, 6 Challenge: 4, 7, 8 Strategic: 1, 9, 10

Assign each to the director most likely to ask it. Use the lenses from your session 1 board map: duty plus background.

★ Step 3 · assign the askerTomasz Wexley (audit and risk): 3, 5, 6 Leonie Achterberg (operations): 4, 7, 8 Halvard Lind (CFO): 2, 5 Ingrid Ashdown (chair): 1, 9, 10

Keep five. The ones most likely to be asked, plus any whose wrong answer would change the vote. Merge near-duplicates.

★ Step 4 · the fiveKeep: 3 (CRM lists), 5 (310 figure), 4 (one-off clean), 8 (depot time), 9 (key accounts) Folded: 2 into 5; 7 into 4; 1 and 10 go to the chair call

Write a two-sentence answer to each, with the number and its source. The answer first, then the evidence. No answer longer than you could say in twenty seconds.

★ Step 5 · two sentences eachQ5 Where does the 310 figure come from? A About 310 credit notes last quarter were for wrong-address deliveries. Source: billing records, in the appendix. Q4 Why not a one-off clean? A We weighed it, and it drifts back within a year. The three systems keep writing their own copies, so the gap returns.

Write the honest "I don't know yet" for the one you cannot answer. What you know, what you will find out, by when, in writing.

★ Step 6 · the honest gapQ9 Will this keep our key accounts? A I don't know yet. Retention is plausible but not measured, and the paper says so. I will bring a proposed measure, with an owner, to the next meeting; Delphine will log it as an action.

Plan the pre-wiring call with the chair: three lines. What the item needs, the questions you expect, and whether the board is ready to decide.

★ Step 7 · the chair callNeeds: a decision on €1.4m; confirm "for decision" is right. Expecting: Leonie on depot time; Tomasz on the CRM lists. Readiness: is retention-not-measured acceptable for a vote tonight? (Constructed. Not a request for support: a check on the item.)

Send nothing new on the night. If writing the answers shows the paper is missing something material, fix the paper before Delphine circulates it, not with a handout in the room. The directors' preparation time is part of their care duty, and a late page takes it away.

Homework

Before session 6

30 min
  1. Finish your five questions with two-sentence answers, each with a number and its source, and one honest "I don't know yet" with a date.
  2. Read your paper once as each director and mark every "compared with what?", "how would we know early?" and "why not?" the paper does not yet answer. Fix the paper, not the script.
  3. Book the pre-wiring call with your chair or committee chair, and the company secretary, before the pack deadline. Use your three lines.
  4. Find your board's action log or the actions schedule in the last minutes you can see. Note how actions are worded: owner, date, and what counts as done.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Tomasz Wexley asks Sunniva: "How do we know the CRM account lists will be opened up?" Which kind of question is it?

"How do we know?" is the signature of an assurance question: it asks for evidence and control, not for a different choice or a long-term view. It is a board question because the main risk of the project sits on it. The typology is a teaching aid, but this one is clear.

2 · A director asks for a figure you do not have. What is the best response?

A guessed number enters the minutes and returns at the next meeting. Deflecting leaves the director's question unanswered, and an informal word reaches one person, not the board. The ICSA/CGI minute-taking guidance records actions with an owner and a date, reviewed under matters arising.

3 · What is pre-wiring the chair for?

The chair runs a well-informed decision, not a campaign. The call confirms the item is set up right, surfaces the questions the chair expects, and tests readiness. Lobbying for the vote undermines the independent judgment each director owes the company.
