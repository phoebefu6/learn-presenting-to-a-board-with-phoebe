Learn Presenting to a Board with Phoebe · Session 1 of 6

What a board is for

A board does not run the company. It sets direction, watches the people who do run it, and answers for both. Tonight we start there, because every question you will be asked in the boardroom comes from that job: what the law and the governance code hold a director accountable for, what that makes them want to know, and whether your item asks them to decide, to discuss or only to note. By the end you have a one-page board map for your own item.

Where we start

The running case is constructed. Part 1 · covers the board's role, oversight and the governance code

Oversight, not management

5 min live

Management runs the company every day. The board meets a few times a year to set the direction, decide the things reserved to it, and hold management to account for the rest. A data leader who walks in expecting a steering committee will pitch at the wrong level: too much detail about how, not enough about why, at what risk, and how the board will know it worked.

LiveWhat the governance code says the board is for3 min▶

The UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC, 2024, read at source) opens with the board: a company should be "led by an effective and entrepreneurial board, whose role is to promote the long-term sustainable success of the company" (Principle A). Non-executive directors "should provide constructive challenge, strategic guidance, offer specialist advice and hold management to account" (Principle H), and should "scrutinise and hold to account the performance of management" (Provision 13).

Internationally, the G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023, read at source) put the same idea in one line of Chapter V: the framework should ensure "the effective monitoring of management by the board". Monitoring is the operative word. Your paper is one of the ways the board monitors management, which is why it is read for what it reveals about how well the company is run, not only for the proposal on top.

Self-studyComply or explain, and who the Code applies to3 min▶

The Code "does not set out a rigid set of rules; instead, it offers flexibility through 'comply or explain' reporting against the Provisions" (FRC 2024, Introduction, read at source). It applies to companies in the UK listing categories for commercial companies and closed-ended investment funds. Constructed Fernhollow Freight example: Fernhollow is not listed, so the Code is not binding on it; its board follows the Code as good practice, which many private and public bodies do. The statute in Part 2 applies to Fernhollow regardless.

Outside the UK, local law and codes differ. The OECD Principles are the shared international reference used on these pages; check your own jurisdiction's companies law before quoting a duty in a paper. Why data in particular is a board matter is taught in Data Governance, Why data governance is a board issue ↗.

Real world

Sign-off is a warning sign. The FRC's 2024 guidance to the Code lists risk factors for poor board decisions (Figure 3, read at source). Two of them are about you: "Poor quality papers" and "a tendency to bring matters to the board for sign-off rather than debate". A board that only signs off is not overseeing, and a paper written to be signed off invites exactly that.

Part 2 · covers directors' duties and the questions they produce

What a director answers for, and so what they ask

5 min live

Directors' questions are not curiosity. Each one traces back to a duty the director personally carries. Read the duties once and most of the questions on the night stop being surprises.

LiveThree sections of the Companies Act, in their own words3 min▶

Section 172: a director must act in the way they consider, "in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole", having regard, among other matters, to six factors: "the likely consequences of any decision in the long term", the interests of employees, relationships with suppliers and customers, the impact on the community and the environment, the company's reputation for high standards, and acting fairly between members. Section 173: "A director of a company must exercise independent judgment." Section 174: "A director of a company must exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence." (All read at source on legislation.gov.uk.)

Section 174 judges a director against two yardsticks: the knowledge, skill and experience "that may reasonably be expected" of someone in that role, and "the general knowledge, skill and experience that the director has". A reading, not the statute's words: the second limb only raises the bar, so a director with a data background is held to more on a data paper, not less.

LiveWhy "withhold" never means hiding a bad truth2 min▶

Session 3 is about leaving detail out of the pack, and it is easy to hear that as permission to leave out bad news. It is the opposite. The OECD Principles say board members "should act on a fully informed basis, in good faith, with due diligence and care" (V.A) and "should have access to accurate, relevant and timely information" (V.F). The Code asks the chair to ensure directors "receive accurate, timely and clear information" (Principle F). A director's care duty assumes the paper can be relied on. Leaving out operational detail helps them; leaving out a material problem takes away the thing their duty depends on, and it is usually found later, by the person who asks Tomasz's kind of question.

Real world

Ask the board's own questions first. The FRC's 2024 guidance gives boards example questions for significant decisions (paragraph 30, read at source), among them "Does the board have a clear idea of the success criteria related to a particular decision?" A paper that already answers that question has done the director's preparation for them.

Part 3 · covers agenda item types: decision, discussion, assurance, noting

For decision, for discussion, or for noting

5 min live

Every agenda item tells the board what kind of attention it needs. Get the type wrong and the meeting goes wrong in a predictable way: a decision slipped in as "for noting" never really gets decided, and a "for decision" item with nothing to decide wastes the board's scarcest resource, its time.

LiveWhere the labels come from, and why they are only labels3 min▶

Neither the Companies Act nor the Code lists agenda item types; they are company-secretary practice. The Chartered Governance Institute's minute-taking guidance (2017, read at source) describes papers "received for noting and no decision is required", and adds that "Some organisations have a policy that papers should not routinely come to the board for 'noting' as the board should be active". The Good Governance Institute's cover-sheet note (2026, read at source) asks every paper to name the board's role: decision, discussion, assurance, or noting, "used sparingly".

The practical test is GGI's: is the board being asked "to decide, shape or test"? If none of the three, ask the company secretary whether the item belongs on the agenda at all, or in a reading room or an appendix.

Self-studyBoard or committee?2 min▶

Many data leaders meet the board first through a committee: audit, risk, or audit and risk combined. A committee does deeper work on a narrower brief and reports to the board, which keeps the decision. Constructed Fernhollow Freight example: Tomasz's audit and risk committee will want the data-protection and control questions about the customer record, often before the board meeting; the full board decides the spend. Ask the company secretary which body sees your item first and in what order, and write for that body's brief.

Build-along

A board map for your own item

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Pick one item you expect to take to a board or committee in the next six months. If you have none, use Sunniva's. You will name the item and its type, say why it is at the board at all, write the one-sentence ask in board language, and list the lens each director brings. It fits on one page, and every later session builds on it.

Name the item and the body. Board or committee, and which meeting. One line, no adjectives.

★ Step 1 · the itemItem: Shared customer record, €1.4 million over two years Body: Fernhollow Freight board, December meeting (Constructed Fernhollow Freight example. Your item will differ.)

Choose the type. Ask what you need from the board: decide, shape, test or know. If the answer is "know", ask whether it needs agenda time at all.

★ Step 2 · the typeType: For decision Test: the board approves or rejects a stated proposal tonight

Say why it is at the board. A reserved matter, a spend above a delegated limit, a principal risk, a regulatory duty. If you cannot say why, the company secretary will ask.

★ Step 3 · why hereWhy here: above the CEO's delegated limit, so reserved to the board. Already: approved by the executive on 14 November.

Write the ask in board language. "The board is asked to ..." followed by one verb and one object. Management detail goes nowhere near this sentence.

★ Step 4 · the askThe board is asked to approve capital spend of €1.4 million over two years for one shared customer record across sales, the depots and billing.

List each director's lens, from their duty and their background. One question per director, in their words, not yours.

★ Step 5 · the lensesIngrid (chair): Is the board ready to decide today? Tomasz (audit and risk): What could go wrong, and how would we know? Leonie (operations): What happens at the eleven depots? Marguerite (CEO): already for it; will be asked if she is sure Halvard (CFO): will be asked whether the cost table holds

Write what the board is NOT being asked. The vendor choice, the sprint plan, the field mapping. Naming it now stops it creeping into the paper in session 2.

★ Step 6 · not the board's jobNot for the board: which matching tool, the build sequence, the field-by-field mapping, team staffing. These are management's, reported back as outcomes.

Check it with one person. Show the map to the company secretary or whoever owns the board agenda. They will tell you within a minute whether the type and the "why here" are right, and that conversation is the first step of the pre-wiring in session 5.

Homework

Before session 2

30 min
  1. Finish your board map for one real item: item, body, type, why here, the ask, the lenses, and what is not the board's job.
  2. Find your organisation's reserved matters or delegated authorities, and the limit that sends your item upstairs. If it is not written down, ask the company secretary.
  3. Read the last set of board minutes you are allowed to see. Count the items by type. How many "for noting" items took real time?
  4. Write one sentence on why your item is not something management can decide alone. If you cannot, it may not be a board item.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Which best describes the board's job, in the words of the UK Corporate Governance Code and the OECD Principles?

Principle A gives the board the long-term success of the company; Principle H asks non-executives for constructive challenge and to hold management to account; OECD Chapter V speaks of effective monitoring of management. Running operations is management's job, and the FRC guidance names sign-off rather than debate as a risk factor.

2 · A director with twenty years in data analytics reads a data paper. Under section 174 of the Companies Act, what standard of care applies?

Section 174(2) has two limbs: the knowledge reasonably expected of someone in the role, and the knowledge this director has. The second limb raises the standard for an expert; it never lowers it below the first.

3 · Sunniva needs the board to approve a €1.4 million spend. Which agenda type fits, and why?

The executive's approval does not settle a matter reserved to the board; the board still has to decide. Discussion and assurance are real types, but neither produces the approval Sunniva needs. Noting would leave the spend undecided.
